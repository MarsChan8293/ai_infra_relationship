## Discovery lessons

### Governance scope is the important variable

Volcano is the clean success case because its community repository maintains a canonical current-maintainer table. LiteLLM is the opposite: CODEOWNERS is useful first-party evidence, but its rules are path-scoped. CUTLASS exposes collaborator/member signals through PR metadata, which is weaker still. The executor therefore records all three differently instead of flattening them into a single maintainer relation.

### Research groups should expose curated people, not every member

HAN Lab and SyFI both have much larger member lists than the graph needs. DISCOVER selects current people whose official bios/publications directly intersect inference, serving, scheduling, long-context systems, or AI infrastructure. This keeps the graph useful without turning a lab roster into a phone book.

### Project discovery benefits from first-party research pages

Catalyst's official Research page gives a high-signal project frontier. FlexFlow Serve, XGrammar and MPK cover distributed serving, structured generation and compiler/runtime mega-kernelization, while already bridging to existing FlashInfer / compiler networks.
